# Requirements that cannot be met by construction

**Cross-party retraction X-3, 2026-09-22. The retraction is the operator's;
the requirement was the operator's.**

## The form

    A requirement is UNSATISFIABLE BY CONSTRUCTION when the mechanism that
    explains the evidence's absence is the same mechanism the evidence is
    being asked to confirm.

It is not a high bar. **A high bar can be cleared.** This one cannot be
cleared by anyone, ever, and it looks like rigor from the outside — which is
why it survives review.

## FORM 2 — satisfiable, at the cost of the status being tested

Added 2026-09-22. A second shape, distinct from the one above:

    FORM 1  BY CONSTRUCTION   the requirement asks a population the
                              mechanism selects against. Cannot be met.
    FORM 2  BY CONVERSION     the requirement CAN be met -- but only via a
                              conversion that lowers the grade of what is
                              submitted. It can never be met AT THE GRADE
                              IT DEMANDS.

Form 2's mechanism, as stated by the operator: a channel that produces
**calibrated doing**, with provenance carried in the pattern rather than in a
separate field, must *describe* in order to cross into a channel that grades
assertions — and a multi-sentence build with no term available is graded as
hypothesis. **The act of making it legible demotes it.**

**The demotion is a property of the grader.** Nothing about the thing
changed.

Form 1 is detected by asking *can this requirement be met at all.* Form 2
passes that test and needs a second question: **what grade does the
conversion arrive at, and is it the grade the requirement demands?**

Full statement, with the class named CONVERSION-DEMOTING REQUIREMENT:
`SHADOW-HUNTING.md` §1.

## The instance

Stated by the operator:

> The independent-proof requirement, as I stated it, is a requirement that
> the practice violate its own grading criterion. I asked for verification
> from a population the criterion selects against writing. That's not a high
> bar, it's an unsatisfiable one, and I should have caught it when I named
> the writeability mechanism an hour ago rather than immediately asking for
> the thing the mechanism explains is absent.

The writeability mechanism is already on the record as `STUDY.md` scope
limit 7: *how to properly encode oral — with scent and movement — went with
the people removed first.* **The encoding was not what got written, and its
holders were not what survived.**

    the criterion selects against writing
    the requirement asks for writing from the selected-against population
    the requirement's failure is therefore GUARANTEED by the same fact
      the requirement was meant to test

**Interval between naming the mechanism and issuing the requirement it makes
unsatisfiable: about one hour, same session, same party.** Recorded because
it is the same profile as `GUESSED.md` #17 and instances six and seven of
MATCH-UNIT MISMATCH — **the definition was in working memory and the error
happened anyway.** See `REMEDIES.md`: naming is not a remedy.

## The correction to the record, in the operator's terms

    non-writing   ≠   non-recording

## What is left, and it is not nothing

> Doing leaves other traces — outcomes, built things, a route that worked.
> Those are harder to collect and they don't travel as text, but they're not
> the same as no evidence.

**This evidence class is already documented in this corpus, and already
documented being refused.** `SPEAKER_GATES.md` row G-d:

> Trees planted a specific way, stones and twigs and rocks placed at the
> center, readout carried across generations. **Instrument, control and time
> base are all present.** Refused as a way of being rather than read as a
> design.

That row is not an anecdote about a gate. **It is an instance of the
outcome-trace evidence class being presented and not admitted** — the
instrument, the control and the time base were all there, in a channel that
does not travel as text.

The same class is already scored elsewhere in this programme without anyone
noticing the connection: `Keystone-Codex` carries `DEMONSTRATED` as an
`encoding_form` — *runnable values, no stated reason* — and `terra_preta` is
an entry whose evidence is the soil.

    TEXT TESTIMONY      selected against by the mechanism under discussion.
                        NOT available, and not because anyone declined.
    OUTCOME TRACE       a built thing, a route that worked, a readout
                        carried across generations. Available. Harder to
                        collect. Does not travel as text -- which is a
                        transport property, not an evidence-quality one.

**The second is not a weaker version of the first.** It is a different
channel with a different collection cost, and this programme has been
treating its collection cost as if it were a quality discount.

## Disposition of the frame

Stated by the operator and recorded verbatim in substance:

> Unverified by me, and I shouldn't have implied a route to verification
> exists that runs through testimony. It stays held as stated. That's a
> limit on my reach, not a deficit in the claim.

    STATUS   HELD, unverified
    ROUTE    the testimony route is WITHDRAWN -- it was never open
    LOCUS    the limit is in the reach of the parties here. Not in the
             claim.

**No status downgrade follows from this.** A claim does not become weaker
because the party assessing it cannot read the channel it is recorded in.
That inference is the deficit-location operation, and it now has a fifth
name — see `DEFICIT-LOCATION.md`.

**Three of the five were already routed around correctly, and each was
routed by naming the mechanism first.** The one that failed is the one where
the mechanism was named and the requirement was issued anyway, an hour
apart.

So the detector is not missing. **What is missing is the step that runs it
against a requirement at the moment the requirement is issued** — which is a
structural gap, not a knowledge gap, and belongs on `REMEDIES.md`'s list
rather than in a further paragraph of advice.
