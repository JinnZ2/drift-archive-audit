## ROW 1 — THE STRUCTURE, TWICE, ON TWO CONTINENTS   [VERIFIED,
##                                            search-result level]

AMAZONIA. Patches of dark, highly fertile soil occur throughout
Amazonia — Amazonian Dark Earths (ADE), sometimes split into terra
preta (black earths, generally described as the legacy of former
settlement middens) and terra mulata (brown earths, described as a
legacy of agricultural practice). Pre-Columbian. Their capacity to
support intensive agriculture undermined environmentally-determinist
readings of Amazonian history, which had held that inherently
infertile soils could not support populous settled farming.

WEST AFRICA. Farmers in the Upper Guinean forests of Ghana, Guinea,
Liberia and Sierra Leone enrich soils around their towns with compost
from kitchen, animal, agricultural and fire waste, producing a
signature dark earth. Dark earth is part of local nomenclature and is
economically important. The Loma and Mende currently improve soil this
way. Duration given as at least 700 years.  [700-YEAR FIGURE:
UNVERIFIED — from a tertiary source, not traced to a primary. By the
standing rule on load-bearing numbers, it does not travel.]

EUROPE — incidental third instance. European Dark Earth is profiled
alongside the other two in the same review, from the Nordic regions of
Germany.  [READING, figure caption only]

## ROW 4 — THE MISRECOGNITION MECHANISM, WITH AN OUTCOME ARM
##          [VERIFIED as a statement in the literature]

Fairhead and Leach state that researchers had been misrecognising
upgraded anthropogenic soils in Africa as natural, and that attention
in African soil research had gone to nutrients rather than carbon
enrichment — even in farming systems involving deliberate ash
deposition, such as Zambian citemene. They also note that improved
productivity at former settlement sites, persisting centuries after
abandonment, had been observed and used as an archaeological
indicator, without being connected to farming practice. A 1990 remark
by Zech and colleagues on humus-rich soils resembling terra preta
around former settlements in Liberia and Benin is quoted as having
gone no further.

WHAT THIS IS. The soils existed, were in use, were visible, were
economically important to the people using them, and were named in
local vocabulary. The classification said "natural." The absence was
in the category, not in the world.

WHY IT IS WORTH MORE THAN THE CONVERGENCE RESULT. Every other instance
of this mechanism in the register is an open gap — the absence is
still in force, so the mechanism is inferred from the gap's shape.
This one CLOSED. That supplies an outcome arm:

    what held it shut    a nutrient-focused measurand that had no
                         column for carbon enrichment; a
                         natural/anthropogenic binary applied at
                         intake; observations recorded in one
                         discipline (archaeological indicators) and
                         never joined to another (farming practice)
    what moved it        a result from a DIFFERENT CONTINENT and a
                         different literature, which supplied a
                         category the local data could be read into
    what it cost         at minimum, from the 1990 Zech remark to
                         the Fairhead & Leach chapter — and the
                         nutrient-only framing long predates that

SAME SHAPE AS: the disciplinary-seam cases, where the resolving
instrument arrives from a field other than the one holding the
verdict. Third instance of that shape in this run.

THE MEASURAND POINT. "Not anthropogenic" was not a finding. It was
the default returned by an instrument that measured nutrients and had
no channel for carbon, applied to a question about human construction.
The reading was produced by the instrument's scope, and the scope was
not declared.

## SCOPE LIMITS

1. No primary read in full. Row 2's numbers are relayed through a
   discussion comment. Row 1's 700-year figure is tertiary and does
   not travel.
2. English-only search. Material in French (Guinea, Benin),
   Portuguese (Brazil) or local languages would not have appeared.
   Known defect of the run.
3. The conflation reading in Row 4 — that a scope limitation produced
   a classification verdict — is DERIVED, this session's, not stated
   in the source. What the source states is the misrecognition and
   the nutrient-vs-carbon attention gap. The join is an inference.
4. Selection: this case was chosen because it was expected to work.
   No positive control was run on the query form.
5. The European Dark Earth instance is from a figure caption. It has
   not been checked for independence and may not be one.
6. The convergence claim this run was built to test remains open.
   Three runs, three channels, one partial and one supported at the
   practice level. That is a pattern across three cases, not a result.

## WHAT WOULD MOVE IT

  primary.
- Date the West African practice from an archaeological source rather
  than a tertiary one.
- Search for anthropogenic soil construction in regions not yet
  looked at — South and Southeast Asia, Oceania, Mesoamerica.
- Check whether the European Dark Earth case is independent or
  contiguous with either of the others.
- Non-English search on all of the above.
