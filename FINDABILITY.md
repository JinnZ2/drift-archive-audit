## What this does to the binding constraint — a third job, done invisibly

`STUDY.md` declares the constraint as **a second party who holds the
speaker's senses**, and last turn split it into two jobs:

    CHECK    five tasks. Verify a result.
    DEFINE   one task. State a measurand that does not exist yet.

**There is a third, and it has been running all session without being logged
once:**

    FIND     locate an instrument that already exists, on the other side
             of a vocabulary boundary, for a party who has no term to
             search with.

Four of this audit's derivations depended on it. **Zero appear on the
constraint list.** They were recorded as findings rather than as constraint
hits, which is why the constraint read as five tasks when it has been
operating on at least nine.

**And FIND has a property the other two do not:** it needs a party fluent in
*both* vocabularies. That is `SPEAKER_GATES` scope limit 1 — the sender
selected on the capacity to translate into the receiver's frame at all —
doing a job nobody assigned it.

    CHECK    needs a party (reader-side or object-side; substitutable)
    DEFINE   needs THE party who holds the sense
    FIND     needs a party who holds BOTH vocabularies

What the chain does establish without any unification: **searching by name
failed here in at least two independent ways** — once for a nameless concept,
once for a named tool nobody had a reason to search for.

## What follows, and it is a change of target

**If the constraint is findability rather than instrument design, then
building more instruments is the wrong response to a gap** — and this session
has built four today.

The cheaper move, in each case, would have been to ask whether the corpus
already held one. **That question was never asked**, in a session whose
explicit subject is a corpus and which had read-only access to it throughout.

    what this record has been optimising   instrument QUALITY
    what the four instances say is scarce  instrument FINDABILITY across
                                           a vocabulary boundary

**Untested as a general claim.** n = 4, one corpus, one pair, and the finder
was the same party in all four — which is the `GUESSED.md` #19 population
defect, flagged here at statement time rather than after a falsification.

## The one check that is cheap and has not been run

Before the next instrument is built in this programme:

    search the corpus for an existing one, using the operator's
    vocabulary rather than the audit's.

That cannot be done by the audit alone — by construction, since not having
the vocabulary is the condition being described. **It is a FIND task, and it
is the fourth entry on a list that had none.**
